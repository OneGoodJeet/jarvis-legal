Privacy Policy

Effective November 13, 2026 (what Tempest writes to Apple Health, workout routes, the health profile backup, the camera and microphone for food, and the Apple Watch app added; the packaged foods download from GitHub added November 12; Friends & challenges, challenge reactions, invites, shared recipe links, the consent record and how the food coach uses your health profile added November 9; the coach's on-device Apple Health trend summary added November 8)

Who we are

Tempest ("the app", "we") is made by Big Yesh LLC, Arizona, United States. Questions about this policy or your data: bigyeshllc@shatisand.com.

Tempest is available in the United States and is for people 18 and older. How we handle health data is also covered in our Consumer Health Data Policy, which is part of this policy.

The short version

  • Your health, fitness, habits, tasks and notes are yours. We use them only to run the app for you.
  • We collect health data only after you agree to it in the app, and you can withdraw that consent at any time in Settings.
  • We never sell your data, never use it for advertising, and never share it with data brokers.
  • Health data from Apple Health is never used for advertising or marketing, never shared with third parties for those purposes, and never stored in iCloud.
  • The app has no ads and no ad trackers. It counts a few anonymous usage events, which you can turn off in Settings.
  • The AI food coach is off until you turn it on. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence it runs entirely on your iPhone, and nothing you ask it leaves the device. It can read a weekly summary of your Apple Health trends there, which never leaves your iPhone.
  • Friends & challenges is off until you turn it on. If you join a step challenge, the people in it see one number from you: your daily step total for that challenge's days. Nothing else from Apple Health is shared.
  • You can delete your account and all of its data from inside the app at any time.

What we collect

Account. You sign in with Apple. We receive a user ID from Apple and, if you choose to share it, your email address (which may be an Apple private relay address). We don't receive your name or your Apple password.

Health and fitness data you allow. With your permission, the app reads from Apple Health things such as weight, steps, sleep, heart rate, heart rate variability, resting heart rate and workouts, plus daily active minutes and stairs climbed from your iPhone's Motion & Fitness data. A lifting workout you log in the app is recorded in Apple Health with its type, its start and end times and the active calories your iPhone counts during it. If you turn on Save food to Apple Health, the nutrition of the food you log is saved there too. Deleting the workout or the food in the app removes it from Apple Health. The full list of what Tempest reads and writes is in the Consumer Health Data Policy. You choose exactly which types to share in the Health permission screen and can change it any time in iOS Settings.

What you enter. Workouts and sets, habits and check-ins, tasks, goals, food logs, nutrition targets, notes and other things you type into the app.

What the app works out. Scores and trends calculated from the above, such as Recovery, Energy, Strain, your sleep score, stress and calm minutes, body age and sleep need.

Photos and camera (stay on your iPhone). If you allow it, the Timeline shows the photos you took on a given day. Photos are read on your iPhone and are never uploaded. The camera is used only for food: to scan a barcode, to read a nutrition label or the front of a pack, or to name the foods in a photo of your meal. The picture is read on your iPhone and is never saved or uploaded. Only the foods and numbers you confirm go into your log.

Microphone and speech (stay on your iPhone). When you tap the mic to say what you ate, or to ask the coach a question, your iPhone turns your words into text on the device. The audio is never saved and never sent to us or to Apple. Only the text appears, for you to check before anything is logged or asked.

Purchases. Subscriptions are sold and processed by Apple. We never receive your payment details. Apple tells our server about changes to your Tempest subscription (for example started, renewed, cancelled or refunded), and we keep that subscription record with your account: which plan, its status and when it ends. The app also checks with Apple on your iPhone whether your subscription is active.

Technical data. Records needed to keep the service working and secure: when your data last synced, which app version you run, and, in our database provider's server logs, the IP address and device type of each request. Server logs are kept for a short time (days, not months) for security and troubleshooting.

Crash reports. If you turn on Settings → Send crash reports (it is off unless you turn it on), the app sends crash and freeze reports from iOS to our server (Supabase) so we can fix them. A report holds the app and iOS versions, the iPhone model and where the code stopped. It holds no health data, workouts, food or names. We delete reports after 90 days, and they are deleted with your account. Turn it off any time.

When you contact us. If you email us, or choose to share a crash report from Settings, we receive what you send, including your email address. We use it only to answer you and fix problems.

Friends & challenges. This is off until you turn it on in the app. When you do, we store the name and emoji you choose for friends to see, an invite code, the friends you connect with (only through an invite code or link you or they share; there is no search and no contact upload), the challenges you create or join, the people you block, and any report you file about someone. When you join a step challenge, the app asks for your separate consent, then sends your daily step total from Apple Health for that challenge's days to your account, and the other people in the challenge can see it. We record when you gave that consent. If you send a reaction to someone in a challenge you share, we store it so they can see it, and it's deleted when either of you leaves the challenge, blocks the other or turns Friends & challenges off. Steps typed into Apple Health by hand aren't counted.

Invites. Your account gets an invite code. If you join with a friend's code, we record that your friend invited you, and whether you go on to pay for Pro (to give your friend their free month). Your friend sees only how many people joined with their code and how many earned them a month, never who. We also keep the App Store offer codes we give you and the date each one must be redeemed by.

Recipes you share as a link. When you choose Share as link, we store a copy of that recipe (its name, ingredients, steps and macros per serving) and the name you choose to show, if any. Anyone with the link can see it. We count how many people saved it, without telling you who. Turning the link off hides it; deleting your account deletes it. If you report a recipe, we keep your report and note so we can review it.

Apple Calendar and Reminders (only if you allow them). If you connect your calendar, the app reads your events on your iPhone to show them in your plan, and adds, changes or deletes an event in Apple Calendar only when you ask it to. A small summary of each day (the titles and times of that day's timed events) is stored with your other data. If you turn on Reminders sync, your tasks and your Apple Reminders are kept in step both ways: reminders in the lists you choose become tasks in the app (their title, notes, due date and time, priority and whether they're done), and your tasks are added to Apple Reminders. Those tasks are stored like any other task you enter. Turning sync off stops it and deletes nothing. You can turn off access any time in iOS Settings.

Workout routes. If you allow it, the page for a run, walk, ride or hike recorded by your Apple Watch shows its route on a map. The route is read from Apple Health when you open the page. It is never saved by Tempest, never copied to your account and never sent to us. Apple Maps draws the map, so Apple receives the area shown.

Apple Watch. If you install the Tempest Watch app, your iPhone sends it today's summary, your open workout, your routines and your recent foods, so you can tick a habit, log a set or log a food from your wrist. This goes directly between your iPhone and your Watch.

What we don't collect. We don't store your location or collect your contacts, we read your calendar and reminders only as described above, and we don't use device identifiers for tracking.

Fast food near you. If you tap "Use my location" on this screen, iOS asks for your permission, and the app gets your location once to ask Apple Maps for restaurants nearby. Your location stays in the app's memory while the screen is open: it isn't saved on your iPhone, isn't added to your food log and isn't sent to us. Apple processes the search under Apple's privacy policy. You can type a place or ZIP code instead and never share your location. The menu nutrition comes with the app, so looking at a menu sends nothing.

Food coach (AI). The food coach is optional and off until you turn it on in the app, after a screen that explains what it reads. It uses your question and the recent conversation, plus a summary of what you logged in the app: your food and its calories and macros for the last two weeks, the foods you log most, your nutrition targets, your goal and calorie floor, and the workouts you logged in the app in the last week. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence, it also reads a short summary of your Apple Health trends, worked out on your iPhone: your average sleep, Recovery and steps over the last week, your weight trend (left out if you hide weight in the app) and your training load. It reads that summary only if you've given the app's health-data consent and turned the coach on, and the summary never leaves your iPhone. The coach never uses your daily Apple Health readings. If you set a status in your health profile (such as unwell, pregnant or a heart-rate medicine), the coach is told only that your training is kept easy or capped, or that a calorie deficit is paused, never the status itself. It never uses your name or your email. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence, the coach uses Apple's on-device model: the answer is written on your iPhone, and nothing about the coach is sent to us, to Apple or to anyone else. The conversation is stored only on your iPhone. Notes you ask the coach to remember stay on your iPhone and are read only by the coach on your iPhone. They are never sent to our server or to anyone else.

Food coach on other iPhones. If we offer the coach on iPhones without Apple Intelligence, it works differently, and the app asks for your separate consent before using it. Each time you ask something, the summary above, without the Apple Health trend summary or the training cap from your health profile, which stay on your iPhone, goes through our server (Supabase) to Anthropic, which runs the AI model and returns an answer. We don't store your questions or the answers on our server; we keep only a daily count of questions, to limit use. Anthropic processes the request on our behalf, keeps it only for a limited time for safety monitoring, and doesn't use it to train its models. The coach gives general information about food, not medical advice. Turn it off, or clear the conversation, any time in Settings → Your data → Food coach.

Why we use it

Only to provide the app to you: to show your dashboards, scores, trends and history; to sync your data across reinstalls and devices; to send the reminders you turn on (these are local notifications, scheduled on your iPhone); to answer you when you contact us; to run the invite program (giving free months, and stopping codes from being used to game it); and to keep the service secure. We don't make automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects, and we don't use your data to train AI models.

Where it's stored

Your data is stored on your iPhone. When you're signed in, a copy of what you enter (including your health profile, if you set one), the scores the app works out and your daily Motion summary is stored in your private account on our database provider, Supabase, hosted on Amazon Web Services in the United States. The readings the app reads from Apple Health (such as weight, sleep and heart rate) stay on your iPhone and are not copied to your account; on a new iPhone the app reads them from Apple Health again. The one exception is Friends & challenges: if you join a step challenge, your daily step totals for its days are stored in your account so the people in the challenge can see them. Each account can access only its own data; this is enforced in the database itself. Data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS) and at rest by our hosting provider.

Who we share it with

We share data only with the service providers that run the app on our behalf, under contracts that limit them to doing that:

  • Supabase (database, authentication and server functions)
  • Anthropic (the AI model behind the food coach on iPhones without Apple Intelligence, only if you agree to that separately, and only for the questions you ask; on iPhones with Apple Intelligence nothing goes to Anthropic)
  • Apple (Sign in with Apple, subscriptions, notifications, Siri if you use the app's Siri shortcuts, in which case Apple processes what you say, and Apple Maps if you search for fast food nearby or open a workout route, in which case Apple processes the place searched or the area shown)

Food search and barcode scanning. When you search the food database or scan a barcode, Tempest sends only the words you typed or the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free, open food database, to look up the food's nutrition. No account, name, email or health data is sent. Like any web request, it reveals your device's IP address to Open Food Facts, whose own privacy policy applies to that request. A food you log is saved to your food list on your iPhone and in your private Tempest account, like the rest of your food log.

Packaged foods database. Once after you install the app (on Wi-Fi, or when you ask), Tempest downloads a database of packaged foods, made from the U.S. Department of Agriculture's FoodData Central (public domain), from GitHub, where we host the file. After that, searching it and looking up barcodes in it happen on your iPhone, and nothing is sent. The download sends nothing about you or your account; like any web request, it reveals your device's IP address to GitHub, whose own privacy policy applies to that request.

Anonymous usage counts. To see where people get stuck, the app sends a few anonymous events to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service built for privacy: for example "onboarding finished", "paywall shown", "trial started" or "workout finished", with the app version, iOS version and device model. Each event carries a random id made on your iPhone for this install, which TelemetryDeck hashes again; it is not your account, your email or Apple's device or advertising ids. No health data, food, notes or other content is ever sent, and nothing is used for tracking or advertising. Turn it off any time in Settings → Share anonymous usage.

Shared recipes. A recipe you share as a link is public to anyone who has the link. Nothing else about you is shown with it, apart from the name you chose.

People you choose. If you use Friends & challenges, your friends and the people in your challenges see your chosen name and emoji, the challenges you share with them, and, for a step challenge you joined, your daily step totals for its days and the average the app works out from them. Nobody else can find or see you. You can leave a challenge, remove a friend, block someone or turn Friends & challenges off at any time. If you report someone, the report goes to us, not to them.

We don't sell or rent personal data and we don't share it for advertising (no "sale" or "sharing" in the sense California law uses those words). We may disclose data if the law requires it, and we'll tell you if we're allowed to.

How long we keep it

We keep your data while your account exists. We may delete an account that has no active subscription and hasn't been used for 90 days, with all its data, the same way as if you deleted it yourself; this isn't switched on yet, and we'll update this policy before it is. Crash reports you choose to send are deleted after 90 days. When you leave a challenge, your step totals for its days are deleted unless another challenge of yours covers them; when you turn off Friends & challenges, your profile, friends, memberships and every shared step total are deleted. Reports you file are kept while we need them to keep the community safe, and are deleted with your account. Items you delete are removed from the app straight away and permanently erased from our servers within 100 days. When you delete your account, your account and all synced data are erased from our live systems immediately, and from our provider's backups within 30 days as those backups expire. If a backup is ever restored in that time, we delete your data from it again.

Your choices and rights

  • Withdraw consent: Settings → Your data → Health data consent. The app stops reading and syncing health data straight away. We keep a record of when you give or withdraw consent (the date, the version of the consent and which you did), so we can show what you agreed to.
  • Delete your account: Settings → Delete account. This erases your account and all synced data, revokes Sign in with Apple, and clears the app's data on that iPhone. It doesn't touch data stored in Apple Health.
  • Food coach: Settings → Your data → Food coach turns it off (nothing more is read or sent) and clears the conversation.
  • Export your data: Settings → Data & export.
  • Change permissions: in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security, or the Health app → Sharing.

US state privacy rights. Depending on where you live (for example California, Washington, Nevada, Connecticut, Colorado, Virginia or Texas), you may have the right to know what personal data we have about you and get a copy, to correct it, to delete it, to withdraw consent, and to opt out of sale, targeted advertising and profiling (we don't do any of those). We won't treat you differently for using these rights. To make a request, use the in-app tools above or email bigyeshllc@shatisand.com from the address on your account. We may need to confirm the request comes from you. We'll answer within 45 days, and may extend that once by 45 days if we tell you why. An authorized agent may make a request for you with your signed permission.

Appeals. If we decline your request, you can appeal by replying to our answer with "Appeal" in the subject. We'll respond within 45 days and explain our decision. If you're still unhappy, you can contact your state attorney general.

Children

Tempest is for adults 18 and older. It isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you think someone under 18 has given us data, contact us and we'll delete it.

Health information

Tempest is a wellness and fitness app, not a medical device. Scores such as Recovery, Energy, Strain and sleep are estimates to help you notice trends. They don't diagnose or treat any condition. Heart notifications shown in Tempest come from your Apple Watch through Apple Health; Tempest only displays them. Tempest isn't covered by HIPAA, which is why we've written our own Consumer Health Data Policy.

Security and breaches

We use encryption, database rules that keep every account separate, and access limited to what's needed to run the service. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your data, we'll tell you, and the regulators the law requires, without unreasonable delay.

Changes

If we change this policy we'll update the effective date above. For significant changes, especially to how we handle health data, we'll tell you in the app before they take effect and, where the law requires, ask for your consent again.
