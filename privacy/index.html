Privacy Policy

Effective November 24, 2026 (health-data consent version 14 and Coach consent version 8: a pause you set is now kept with your account; Coach now runs on Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, and reads the food you log, including photos of a meal or a label; an answer read aloud is voiced by OpenAI; what Coach knows about you and your coach chats are kept with your account; counts of which screens and features you use are kept with your account, and we look at totals of what people log; period days, State of Mind, body measurements, progress photos, in-app feedback, friend notifications and what we keep after a referral added)

Who we are

Tempest ("the app", "we") is made by Big Yesh LLC, Arizona, United States. Questions about this policy or your data: bigyeshllc@shatisand.com.

Tempest is available in the United States and is for people 18 and older. How we handle health data is also covered in our Consumer Health Data Policy, which is part of this policy.

The short version

  • Your health, fitness, habits, tasks and notes are yours. We use them to run the app for you and, in the limited way described under "How we learn what to improve", to make the app better.
  • We collect health data only after you agree to it in the app, and you can withdraw that consent at any time in Settings.
  • We never sell your data, never use it for advertising, and never share it with data brokers.
  • Readings from Apple Health stay on your iPhone and are never sent to our servers, except the body details your targets use (such as height and weight), daily step totals for a step challenge you join, past food you ask the app to copy in, and, only if you turn on Coach, the short summary of your trends that Coach reads (described under Coach below). They are never used for advertising or marketing, and never shared with anyone for those purposes.
  • The app has no ads and no ad trackers. It counts which screens and features are used, anonymously and with your account, which you can turn off in Settings.
  • Coach, the app's AI coach, is off until you turn it on. It is powered by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. When you ask it something, or log food by typing, speaking or taking a photo while it is on, your question and the summaries it reads are sent through our server to Anthropic to write the answer. Anthropic's terms bar it from training its models on what is sent and require it to delete it within 30 days, unless it has to keep something longer to comply with the law or to investigate misuse of its service.
  • Friends & challenges is off until you turn it on. If you join a step challenge, the people in it see one number from you: your daily step total for that challenge's days. Nothing else from Apple Health is shared.
  • You can delete your account and all of its data from inside the app at any time.

What we collect

Account. You sign in with Apple. We receive a user ID from Apple and, if you choose to share it, your email address (which may be an Apple private relay address). If you share your name, the app keeps only your first name, on your iPhone, to greet you; it is never sent to us. We don't receive your name or your Apple password.

Health and fitness data you allow. With your permission, the app reads from Apple Health things such as weight, steps, sleep, heart rate, heart rate variability, resting heart rate, workouts and their routes, period days and mood, plus your minutes walking, running and cycling and the floors you climb from your iPhone's Motion & Fitness data. Tempest writes to Apple Health only what you log or do in the app: your workouts (with their type, times, the active calories your iPhone counts, a distance you type and how hard they felt), your weigh-ins, your mood as State of Mind, the breathing minute as Mindful Minutes, and, if you turn on Save food to Apple Health, the nutrition of the food you log (including vitamins and minerals) and the water you log. Deleting the workout or the food in the app removes it from Apple Health. The full list of what Tempest reads and writes is in the Consumer Health Data Policy. You choose exactly which types to share in the Health permission screen and can change it any time in iOS Settings.

What you enter. Workouts and sets, habits and check-ins, tasks, goals, food logs and the meals you plan, nutrition targets, your mood, body measurements you take with a tape (waist, chest, hips, upper arm, thigh and neck), notes and other things you type into the app.

About you. If you enter them, your sex, age, height, weight, activity level and eating style, used for your targets and scores. Your health profile, if you set one: a status (pregnant, postpartum, taking a heart-rate medicine, or unwell, with the days it started and ended) and the sex you choose, used only to keep your training and calorie targets safe. Both are backed up to your private account so a new iPhone gets them back. Your date of birth from Apple Health stays on your iPhone; we keep only your age and the day it was true.

What the app works out. Scores and trends calculated from the above, such as Energy, Strain, your sleep score, stress and calm minutes, body age and sleep need. Your Energy score, sleep need and suggested bedtime are worked out on your iPhone and are not stored in your account. If Coach is on, today's Energy is among the things sent to write its answers.

Photos and camera. If you allow it, the Timeline shows the photos you took on a given day. Photos from your library are read on your iPhone and are never uploaded. The camera is used for food: to scan a barcode, to read a nutrition label or the front of a pack, or to name the foods in a photo of your meal. With Coach off, that picture is read on your iPhone and is never saved or uploaded. With Coach on, up to three photos of a meal, or a photo of a nutrition label, are sent through our server to Anthropic so Claude can name the foods; the photos are not kept on your iPhone, on our server or in your account, and Anthropic must delete them within 30 days. Either way, only the foods and numbers you confirm go into your log. If you take progress photos, they are kept in the app on your iPhone only: never uploaded or synced, left out of iCloud backup, and deleted if you withdraw health-data consent, sign out or delete your account. They are included in your data export. You can lock them behind Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode.

Microphone and speech. When you tap the mic to say what you ate, or to ask Coach a question, your iPhone turns your words into text on the device. The audio is never saved and never sent to us, to Apple or to anyone else. Only the text appears. With Coach on, that text is sent like anything you type to Coach (see Coach below). When Coach reads an answer aloud in its own voice, only the words of that answer are sent through our server to OpenAI to make the audio: never your question, your data or who you are. With that voice off, your iPhone reads the answer on the device.

Purchases. Subscriptions are sold and processed by Apple. We never receive your payment details. Apple tells our server about changes to your Tempest subscription (for example started, renewed, cancelled or refunded), and we keep that subscription record with your account: which plan, its status and when it ends. The app also checks with Apple on your iPhone whether your subscription is active.

Technical data. Records needed to keep the service working and secure: when your data last synced, which app version you run, and, in our database provider's server logs, the IP address and device type of each request. Server logs are kept for a short time (days, not months) for security and troubleshooting.

Crash reports. If you turn on Settings → Help → Diagnostics → Send crash reports (it is off unless you turn it on), the app sends crash and freeze reports from iOS to our server (Supabase) so we can fix them. A report holds the app and iOS versions, the iPhone model and where the code stopped. It holds no health data, workouts, food or names. We delete reports after 90 days, and they are deleted with your account. Turn it off any time.

When you contact us. If you email us, or choose to share a crash report from Settings, we receive what you send, including your email address. If you send feedback from inside the app (Settings → Help: an idea, a problem or a question), we store what you write, which of the three it is and the app version and build, with your account. If you tick Include app details, the app version, iOS version, iPhone model, sync status, time zone and units are added; no health data. Feedback is kept so we can read it, is included in your data export, and is deleted with your account. We use it only to answer you and to fix and improve the app.

Notifications. Reminders and nudges are local notifications, scheduled on your iPhone. If you use Friends & challenges with its notifications on, we store your iPhone's Apple push token with your account so we can tell you about a new friend, a challenge invite or a reaction. These notifications name the friend and what they did and carry no health data. The token is removed when you turn those notifications off or sign out.

Friends & challenges. This is off until you turn it on in the app. When you do, we store the name and emoji you choose for friends to see, an invite code, the friends you connect with (only through an invite code or link you or they share; there is no search and no contact upload), the challenges you create or join, the people you block, and any report you file about someone. When you join a step challenge, the app asks for your separate consent, then sends your daily step total from Apple Health for that challenge's days to your account, and the other people in the challenge can see it. We record when you gave that consent. If you send a reaction to someone in a challenge you share, we store it so they can see it, and it's deleted when either of you leaves the challenge, blocks the other or turns Friends & challenges off. Steps typed into Apple Health by hand aren't counted.

Invites. Your account gets an invite code. If you join with a friend's code, we record that your friend invited you, and whether you go on to pay for Pro (to give your friend their free month). Your friend sees only how many people joined with their code and how many earned them a month, never who. We also keep the App Store offer codes we give you and the date each one must be redeemed by.

Recipes you share as a link. When you choose Share as link, we store a copy of that recipe (its name, ingredients, steps and macros per serving) and the name you choose to show, if any. Anyone with the link can see it. We count how many people saved it, without telling you who. Turning the link off hides it; deleting your account deletes it. If you report a recipe, we keep your report and note so we can review it.

Recipes from a link. If you paste a recipe's web address into the recipe editor, your iPhone fetches that page directly from its website and reads the recipe on your iPhone. The link doesn't go through us. Like any visit, the website receives your device's IP address, and its own privacy policy applies.

Bringing in your history. If you import a file exported from another app (such as MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It, MacroFactor, Strong, Hevy, Todoist, Streaks, or a weight history), the file is read on your iPhone and what it holds joins your log, which is backed up like anything you enter; an imported weight history is saved to Apple Health. If you ask, Tempest also copies once the past food other apps saved to Apple Health into your food log.

Apple Calendar and Reminders (only if you allow them). If you connect your calendar, the app reads your events on your iPhone to show them in your plan, and adds, changes or deletes an event in Apple Calendar only when you ask it to. A summary of each day's events is kept on your iPhone only; event titles and times are never sent to us. If you turn on Reminders sync, your tasks and your Apple Reminders are kept in step both ways: reminders in the lists you choose become tasks in the app (their title, notes, due date and time, priority and whether they're done), and your tasks are added to Apple Reminders. Those tasks are stored like any other task you enter. Turning sync off stops it and deletes nothing. If you tap Add to Reminders on a meal plan's grocery list, the app writes that list's items to a Reminders list of its own on your iPhone. You can turn off access any time in iOS Settings.

Workout routes and splits. If you allow it, the page for a run, walk, ride or hike recorded by your Apple Watch shows its route on a map, and a run's splits and best times. The route and distance readings are read from Apple Health when you open the page. They are never saved by Tempest, never copied to your account and never sent to us. Apple Maps draws the map, so Apple receives the area shown.

Apple Watch. If you install the Tempest Watch app, your iPhone sends it today's summary, your open workout, your routines and your recent foods, so you can tick a habit, log a set or log a food from your wrist. This goes directly between your iPhone and your Watch.

What we don't collect. We don't store your location or collect your contacts, we read your calendar and reminders only as described above, and we don't use device identifiers for tracking.

Fast food near you. If you tap "Use my location" on this screen, iOS asks for your permission, and the app gets your location once to ask Apple Maps for restaurants nearby. Your location stays in the app's memory while the screen is open: it isn't saved on your iPhone, isn't added to your food log and isn't sent to us. Apple processes the search under Apple's privacy policy. You can type a place or ZIP code instead and never share your location. The menu nutrition comes with the app, so looking at a menu sends nothing.

Coach (AI). Coach is optional and off until you turn it on in the app, after a screen that says it is an AI, what is sent and to whom. Coach is powered by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, PBC (United States). With Coach on, each question goes through our server to Anthropic so Claude can write the answer, together with: your question and the recent chat; a summary of what you logged in the app (your food with its calories and macros for the last two weeks, the foods, recipes and saved meals you log most, your nutrition targets, goal and calorie floor, and this week's workouts with their exercises, sets and weights); a short summary of your Apple Health trends worked out on your iPhone (your average sleep, steps and training load over the last week, your weight trend unless you hide weight, today's Energy and call, last night's sleep, and this week's workouts from Apple Health: the kind, how long and how far, never a heart rate); and, if you set them, the foods you avoid, the notes Coach keeps about you, your care routines, your first name, and today's tasks, habits, goal and calendar events when you ask it to plan your day. Each morning, a shorter summary from the same list is sent to write the note at the top of Today, and short summaries are sent to write the lines Coach shows on the Food, Energy and Sleep pages and in its notifications. With Coach on, the food you log by typing or speaking, and photos of a meal or a nutrition label, are also sent to Anthropic to be read into foods and amounts; the numbers still come from the food database. Coach never receives your other daily Apple Health readings, your date of birth, your email, your account id or your location. If you set a status in your health profile (such as unwell, pregnant or a heart-rate medicine), Coach is told only that your training is kept easy or capped, or that a calorie deficit is paused, never the status itself. Our server passes each request through and records only counts and cost; it does not store or log your questions, the answers or the photos. Anthropic's terms bar it from training its models on what is sent and require it to delete it within 30 days, unless it has to keep something longer to comply with the law or to investigate misuse of its service. When Coach reads an answer aloud in its own voice, the words of that answer go to OpenAI, L.L.C. (United States), whose terms likewise bar training on them and limit how long they are kept to 30 days, with the same exceptions. Both act only on our instructions, under written data-processing terms. What Coach keeps. What Coach knows about you (notes such as an injury, how you like to be coached, your goals), its short notes on your earlier chats, the foods you avoid, your coach chats (the newest 300 lines) and the answers you save are stored on your iPhone and, with Coach on, in your private account on our database, so a new iPhone picks up where you left off. A pause you set in the app (away, sick or a rest week, with its days) is kept the same way: on your iPhone and, with Coach on, in your private account, so a new iPhone keeps it. It is not sent to Anthropic. Our server never reads them and never sends them anywhere on its own. Turning Coach off removes them from your account; Delete my health data and deleting your account remove them too. If Anthropic can't be reached, Apple's on-device model (on iPhones with Apple Intelligence) or the app's own rules write the answer on your iPhone. Every answer is checked on your iPhone and on our server before you see it. Coach's answers are written by an AI and can be wrong. They are general information about food, training, sleep and Energy, not medical advice. Turn it off, or clear the chat, any time in Settings → Connections → Coach.

Why we use it

To provide the app to you and, as described under "How we learn what to improve", to make it better: to show your dashboards, scores, trends and history; to sync your data across reinstalls and devices; to send the reminders you turn on (these are local notifications, scheduled on your iPhone) and, if you use Friends & challenges, its notifications; to read and answer feedback and messages you send us; to run the invite program (giving free months, and stopping codes from being used to game it); and to keep the service secure. We don't make automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects, and we don't use your data to train AI models. Our agreements with Anthropic and OpenAI don't allow them to either.

Where it's stored

Your data is stored on your iPhone. When you're signed in, a copy of what you enter (including your health profile and the details about you, if you set them, your mood and your body measurements), the training numbers the app works out and your daily Motion summary (minutes walking, running and cycling, and floors climbed; time driving or sitting still stays on your iPhone) is stored in your private account on our database provider, Supabase, hosted on Amazon Web Services in the United States. The readings the app reads from Apple Health (such as weight, sleep, heart rate, period days and State of Mind) stay on your iPhone and are not copied to your account; on a new iPhone the app reads them from Apple Health again. So do your Energy score, sleep need and suggested bedtime, your calendar events, and your progress photos. The exceptions are the body details your targets use (such as height and weight, which can come from Apple Health and are backed up with the details about you), your daily step totals for a step challenge you join, which are stored in your account so the people in the challenge can see them, and past food you ask the app to copy in from Apple Health, which becomes part of your food log. What's on your iPhone is part of your own iPhone backup, which you control. Each account can access only its own data; this is enforced in the database itself. Data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS) and at rest by our hosting provider.

Who we share it with

We share data only with the service providers that run the app on our behalf, under contracts that limit them to doing that:

  • Supabase (database, authentication and server functions)
  • Anthropic (only with Coach on: writes Coach's answers and reads the food you log, from what is listed under Coach above; no training on your data; deleted within 30 days as described there)
  • OpenAI (only when Coach reads an answer aloud in its own voice: the words of that answer, nothing else)
  • TelemetryDeck (anonymous usage counts, described below; never your account, name, email or any health reading)
  • Apple (Sign in with Apple, subscriptions, notifications, Siri if you use the app's Siri shortcuts, in which case Apple processes what you say, and Apple Maps if you search for fast food nearby or open a workout route, in which case Apple processes the place searched or the area shown)

Food search and barcode scanning. When you search the food database or scan a barcode, Tempest sends only the words you typed or the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free, open food database, to look up the food's nutrition. No account, name, email or health data is sent. Like any web request, it reveals your device's IP address to Open Food Facts, whose own privacy policy applies to that request. A food you log is saved to your food list on your iPhone and in your private Tempest account, like the rest of your food log.

Packaged foods database. Once after you install the app (on Wi-Fi, or when you ask), Tempest downloads a database of packaged foods, made from the U.S. Department of Agriculture's FoodData Central (public domain), from GitHub, where we host the file. After that, searching it and looking up barcodes in it happen on your iPhone, and nothing is sent. The download sends nothing about you or your account; like any web request, it reveals your device's IP address to GitHub, whose own privacy policy applies to that request.

Anonymous usage counts. To see where people get stuck, the app sends anonymous events to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service built for privacy: for example "onboarding finished", "paywall shown", "trial started", "workout finished" or "food logged" (only where it was logged from, never the food), with the app version, iOS version and device model. Each event carries a random id made on your iPhone for this install, which TelemetryDeck hashes again; it is not your account, your email or Apple's device or advertising ids. Some events say that a feature was used, such as that a weigh-in or a body measurement was saved, but never the value, which habit, experiment or body measurement it was, or any health reading, food, note or other content. Nothing is used for tracking or advertising. It is on unless you turn it off in Settings → Help → Diagnostics → Share usage data; off means nothing is sent at all.

How we learn what to improve. Tempest keeps counts of which screens and features you use and how often, with your account, on our own database. A count is a fixed word such as "opened the Food tab" or "logged food by search"; it never holds a food, a number, a note or an Apple Health reading. We also look at totals of what people log, such as how many meals and workouts and which foods and exercises are most common, and at food searches that found nothing (sent without your account), to decide what to build and fix. Apple Health readings, and food or workouts that came from Apple Health, are never used for this. We don't sell any of it, share it with anyone or use it for ads. Turn it off in Settings → Help → Diagnostics → Share usage data: nothing more is sent, your counts are deleted, and what you log is left out of our totals. We keep the counts for 13 months, then only totals that name no account. They are deleted with your account and are part of Export your data.

Shared recipes. A recipe you share as a link is public to anyone who has the link. Nothing else about you is shown with it, apart from the name you chose.

People you choose. If you use Friends & challenges, your friends and the people in your challenges see your chosen name and emoji, the challenges you share with them, and, for a step challenge you joined, your daily step totals for its days and the average the app works out from them. Nobody else can find or see you. You can leave a challenge, remove a friend, block someone or turn Friends & challenges off at any time. If you report someone, the report goes to us, not to them.

We don't sell or rent personal data and we don't share it for advertising (no "sale" or "sharing" in the sense California law uses those words). We may disclose data if the law requires it, and we'll tell you if we're allowed to.

How long we keep it

We keep your data while your account exists. We may delete an account that has no active subscription and hasn't been used for 90 days, with all its data, the same way as if you deleted it yourself; this isn't switched on yet, and we'll update this policy before it is. Crash reports you choose to send are deleted after 90 days. Feedback you send from the app is kept while your account exists. What Coach knows about you, your coach chats and a pause you set are kept in your account while Coach is on. Usage counts are kept for 13 months. When you leave a challenge, your step totals for its days are deleted unless another challenge of yours covers them; when you turn off Friends & challenges, your profile, friends, memberships and every shared step total are deleted. Reports you file are kept while we need them to keep the community safe, and are deleted with your account. Items you delete are removed from the app straight away and permanently erased from our servers within 100 days. When you delete your account, your account and all synced data are erased from our live systems immediately, and from our backups within 30 days as those backups expire. If a backup is ever restored in that time, we delete your data from it again.

Two records about invites outlive an account, so a free month can't be claimed twice. If a friend's purchase earned you a referral reward, we keep that purchase's App Store transaction number, without anything that identifies the account. If you received a free month through a friend's invite, we keep a one-way scrambled code (a SHA-256 hash) made from your Sign in with Apple identity, with no account id. It can't be turned back into your Apple identity, and it is only compared when someone redeems an invite, never shown or shared.

Your choices and rights

  • Withdraw consent: Settings → Your data → Health data consent. The app stops reading Apple Health and Motion & Fitness and stops syncing straight away. Widgets stop showing your health numbers, booked health notifications are cleared, Coach turns off, and Coach's notes, your coach chats, your foods to avoid and a pause you set are cleared from your iPhone and your account. Step totals you shared in challenges are deleted, the health profile and body details backed up to your account are removed, and progress photos on your iPhone are deleted. We keep a record of when you give or withdraw consent (the date and time our server received it, the version of the consent and which you did), so we can show what you agreed to.
  • Delete your account: Settings → Account → Delete account. This erases your account and all synced data, revokes Sign in with Apple, and clears the app's data on that iPhone. Apple Health keeps its data, including what Tempest saved there, unless you turn on "Also remove workouts, food, weigh-ins, moods and mindful minutes Tempest saved to Apple Health" on the delete screen. Nothing else in Apple Health is touched.
  • Coach: Settings → Connections → Coach turns it off: nothing more is sent to Anthropic or OpenAI, and what Coach knows, your coach chats and a pause you set are removed from your account. You can also clear the chat there.
  • Export your data: Settings → Your data → Export your data.
  • Usage data and crash reports: Settings → Help → Diagnostics.
  • Change permissions: in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security, or the Health app → Sharing.

US state privacy rights. Depending on where you live (for example California, Washington, Nevada, Connecticut, Colorado, Virginia or Texas), you may have the right to know what personal data we have about you and get a copy, to correct it, to delete it, to withdraw consent, and to opt out of sale, targeted advertising and profiling (we don't do any of those). We won't treat you differently for using these rights. To make a request, use the in-app tools above or email bigyeshllc@shatisand.com from the address on your account. We may need to confirm the request comes from you. We'll answer within 45 days, and may extend that once by 45 days if we tell you why. An authorized agent may make a request for you with your signed permission.

Appeals. If we decline your request, you can appeal by replying to our answer with "Appeal" in the subject. We'll respond within 45 days and explain our decision. If you're still unhappy, you can contact your state attorney general.

Children

Tempest is for adults 18 and older. It isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you think someone under 18 has given us data, contact us and we'll delete it.

Health information

Tempest is a wellness and fitness app, not a medical device. Scores such as Energy, Strain and sleep are estimates to help you notice trends. They don't diagnose or treat any condition. Notifications from your Apple Watch shown in Tempest (irregular rhythm, high and low heart rate, hypertension and sleep apnea) come from Apple through Apple Health; Tempest shows them as Apple sent them and never works them out itself. Tempest isn't covered by HIPAA, which is why we've written our own Consumer Health Data Policy.

Security and breaches

We use encryption, database rules that keep every account separate, and access limited to what's needed to run the service. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your data, we'll tell you, and the regulators the law requires, without unreasonable delay.

Changes

If we change this policy we'll update the date above. For significant changes, especially to how we handle health data, we'll tell you in the app before they take effect and, where the law requires, ask for your consent again.
